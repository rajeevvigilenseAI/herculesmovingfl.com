Moving to Fort Lauderdale: A Local Mover's Guide

October 2026  |  Fort Lauderdale, Broward County

Fort Lauderdale is a boat town first. Miles of canals cut through its east side, the Intracoastal Waterway runs parallel to the beach, and plenty of houses come with a dock out back. That water shapes almost everything about living here, including how a moving truck gets from one side of town to the other.

This guide covers the parts of the city people most often move into, the logistics that are specific to Fort Lauderdale, and a few habits that make move day go smoother. If you are heading into a tower on the beach or downtown, read our separate high-rise condo moving guide as well, since building rules deserve their own checklist.

Why the water matters on move day

The nickname "Venice of America" is not just marketing. Canal neighborhoods sit on finger-shaped streets that often end in a dead end at the water. There is usually one way in and one way out, curbside parking is limited, and a full-size truck may need to back down the street or park at the corner. Low-hanging trees and landscaping along the swale are common too.

The bigger timing factor is the drawbridges. The Intracoastal is crossed by bridges at roads such as Sunrise Boulevard, Las Olas Boulevard and the 17th Street Causeway, and these open for boat traffic. A bridge opening can hold traffic for several minutes, and on a busy boating day you may catch more than one. If your move runs from the mainland to the barrier island, or the reverse, build a cushion into the schedule rather than assuming a straight shot.

  • Check whether your route crosses the Intracoastal or the New River.
  • Expect a few extra minutes per crossing, more on weekends when boat traffic is heavier.
  • Ask your movers how they plan to stage the truck on a dead-end canal street.

Neighborhoods people move into

Las Olas and downtown

Las Olas Boulevard is the city's main restaurant and shopping street, and it links downtown to the beach. Downtown itself has grown into a cluster of residential towers along the New River and around the Brightline station. Most moves here are condo or apartment moves with loading docks, elevator reservations and a building manager to coordinate with. Side streets off Las Olas also hold low-rise apartment buildings and townhomes that have their own parking challenges.

Victoria Park

Just northeast of downtown, Victoria Park is a mix of older single-family homes, renovated cottages and small multi-unit buildings on tree-lined streets. Some streets are narrow, and several properties have tight driveways or gates. It is a popular choice for people who want to walk or bike to Las Olas but still have a yard.

Flagler Village

Flagler Village sits north of downtown near the rail corridor. It has shifted from warehouses toward newer mid-rise and high-rise apartments, along with art spaces and breweries. Renters who work downtown are a big part of the crowd. Expect modern buildings with move-in reservations, and street parking that fills up quickly in the evening.

The beach and the barrier island

Along A1A you will find condo towers, older low-rise buildings and a handful of single-family homes on the Intracoastal side. Access means crossing a bridge, and A1A itself can be slow on weekends and during events. Some older beachside buildings have small elevators and no true loading dock, so measure larger furniture before you commit to bringing it.

Canal neighborhoods

Areas such as Rio Vista, Harbor Beach, the Las Olas Isles and Coral Ridge are where many waterfront single-family homes are found. These houses often have long driveways, docks and outdoor living areas, and owners tend to move larger furniture, outdoor sets and marine gear. Plan for patio furniture, grills and dock equipment as part of the inventory, not an afterthought.

Who moves to Fort Lauderdale

The city draws a wide mix. Boaters and yacht crew settle near the marinas and the canals. Young professionals pick downtown and Flagler Village. Families and long-term residents look at the single-family neighborhoods west of US-1. Seasonal residents keep condos near the beach and often move furniture in or out on a schedule. Each of those moves looks different, which is why a one-size plan rarely fits here.

Practical tips for a Fort Lauderdale move

  1. Map your crossings. Note every drawbridge on the route between your old and new address, and pick a start time that avoids rush hour on US-1, I-95 and I-595.
  2. Ask about the street. If your new home is on a canal, find out whether the street is a dead end, whether there is room to turn a truck, and whether there are low trees or wires.
  3. Check HOA and building rules. Condo associations and some townhome communities set move-in hours and may ask for a Certificate of Insurance. Ask early.
  4. Protect the outdoor items. Outdoor furniture, planters and dock gear can scratch or stain other items. Keep them grouped and wrapped separately.
  5. Watch the weather. Afternoon storms are common in summer, and hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. Morning starts help, and our guide to the best time to move in South Florida covers the seasonal tradeoffs.

Special items that come with waterfront living

Waterfront homes tend to hold heavier and more delicate pieces than you might expect: marble tables, artwork, pianos and the occasional gun safe. Frequently Asked Questions

Do drawbridges really affect a move in Fort Lauderdale?

They can. Bridges over the Intracoastal open for boat traffic, and each opening can hold road traffic for several minutes, so routes that cross the water should include some extra time.

Can a moving truck fit on a canal-side street?

Often yes, but many canal streets are narrow dead ends with limited parking. Your movers may need to back in or park at the corner, so mention the street layout when you book.

Do I need a Certificate of Insurance to move into a Fort Lauderdale condo?

Many condo buildings ask for one, along with a reserved elevator and set move hours. Ask your building manager what they need, and we can coordinate the COI with them.

What is the best time of day to move in Fort Lauderdale?

Early morning usually works best. It avoids the worst traffic on I-95, I-595 and US-1, and it gets most of the work done before summer afternoon storms.

Is Victoria Park or Flagler Village better for a first move to the city?

It depends on what you want. Victoria Park leans toward older houses with yards close to Las Olas, while Flagler Village is mostly newer apartments near downtown.
