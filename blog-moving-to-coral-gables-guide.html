Moving to Coral Gables: What to Plan For

October 2026  |  Coral Gables, Miami-Dade County

Coral Gables was laid out as a planned city, and it still looks like one. Barrel-tile roofs, coral rock walls, arched doorways and big banyan and oak trees line streets like Granada Boulevard, Alhambra Circle and the residential blocks around the Biltmore. It is one of the most distinctive places to live in Miami-Dade, and one of the more particular places to move into.

Most of what makes a Gables move different comes down to three things: older homes built for a different era of furniture, streets that were not designed for box trucks, and a city that cares a great deal about how its neighborhoods look and sound. Here is how to plan for each.

Older Mediterranean-style homes

Many Coral Gables homes date from the city's early decades, and plenty of newer ones copy the same Mediterranean Revival style. They are beautiful, and they have quirks that matter on move day.

  • Arched and narrow doorways. Older interior doors are often narrower than modern ones, and an arch takes height off the sides. Measure doors and hallways before deciding where a sectional or armoire will go.
  • Tile and wood floors. Original terrazzo, Cuban tile and hardwood are common. Ask your movers to protect floors along the main carrying path, and point out any tile you know is fragile.
  • Stairs with turns. Two-story homes often have a curved or tight staircase. Large headboards and dressers may need to come apart, and basic disassembly and reassembly is included with Hercules Local Movers.
  • Courtyards and side gates. Some homes are easier to load through a side gate or courtyard than the front door. Walk the property and find the widest, flattest path before move day.

Trees, narrow streets and where the truck goes

The tree canopy is one of the best things about living in the Gables, and one of the biggest planning issues for a moving truck. Low limbs from banyans and live oaks can hang over the street and driveway, and a tall truck can scrape them or simply not fit. Many residential streets are also narrow, curve around traffic circles and plazas, and have no curb parking on one side.

That is why where the truck parks matters more here than in most of the county. A long carry from a spot down the block adds time, and a truck squeezed under low branches risks damage. When you book, share the address and a note about overhanging trees or a tight driveway, and we will plan the parking and loading path around it.

Check the city's rules before move day

Coral Gables has a reputation for paying close attention to how residential streets are used. We cannot list the specific rules for you, and they can change, so check directly with the City of Coral Gables before your move about:

  • Whether a moving truck can park on your street, and for how long
  • Allowed hours for moving and loading work in residential areas
  • Any notice or permit the city expects for blocking part of a street
  • Rules on leaving boxes, packing material or old furniture at the curb

If you live in a condo or a community with its own association, ask the HOA or building manager the same questions. Their rules may be stricter than the city's.

Antiques, art and fine furniture

Gables households tend to have more heirloom pieces than average: antique armoires, inlaid dining tables, gilt mirrors, oil paintings, grandfather clocks and pianos. These need more than a moving blanket thrown over them.

  1. Make a list of the pieces that matter most. Note anything fragile, valuable or irreplaceable when you request an estimate, so the right crew and materials come on the day.
  2. Photograph each piece. Take clear photos of condition before the move for your own records.
  3. Empty and secure drawers and doors. Remove loose glass shelves and keys, and tell the crew about any loose veneer or weak joints.
  4. Plan the placement. Decide where heavy pieces go in the new home so they are carried once, not moved again across old floors.

Pianos, fine art, antiques and safes are part of our specialty moving service, which covers how we wrap, carry and place them.

University of Miami moves

The University of Miami campus on the south side of the city brings a steady flow of student and faculty moves. They look very different from a family home move.

Students

Student moves are usually small: a bed, a desk, a dresser and boxes, going to an apartment near campus or along US-1 (South Dixie Highway). They cluster around the start and end of semesters, when everyone wants the same weekend. Book early, keep your lease dates in hand and check move-in rules with your apartment building. A load this size is a good fit for our small moves service, with no minimums.

Faculty and staff

Faculty moves tend to be the opposite: whole households, often with large book collections, home offices and sometimes a piano. Books are heavy, so pack them in small boxes. If you are relocating from out of state into a Gables home, the house logistics above apply, and the residential moving page covers the full-home process.

Getting around on move day

The main routes in and out are US-1 along the south and east, LeJeune Road and Ponce de Leon Boulevard running north and south, and Coral Way and Bird Road running east and west. The Miracle Mile and downtown Gables area gets busy with shoppers, office workers and restaurant traffic, so avoid parking a truck there in the middle of the day if you can. For longer runs, the Palmetto Expressway and the Dolphin Expressway are close by to the west and north.

Early starts help here for the usual reasons - lighter traffic, cooler temperatures - and because a morning finish leaves time to deal with anything unexpected at the house. Summer afternoons bring frequent storms, and hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need permission from Coral Gables to park a moving truck on my street?

It depends on the street and the city's current rules. Check with the City of Coral Gables, and your HOA if you have one, about truck parking and allowed moving hours before your move date.

Will a large moving truck fit on my tree-lined Gables street?

Not always. Low branches and narrow streets are common, so share your address and any tree or driveway concerns when you book and we can plan parking and the loading path around them.

Can you move antiques and a piano into an older Coral Gables home?

Yes. Pianos, antiques and fine art are part of our specialty service, and we plan the carrying path around narrow doors, stairs and older floors.

When is the busiest time for University of Miami student moves?

Student moves cluster around the start and end of semesters. Booking early and confirming your apartment's move-in rules makes those weekends much easier.
