How to Avoid Moving Scams in Florida

July 2026  |  Consumer Safety

Florida moving scams cost residents millions every year. High turnover, seasonal demand, and a flood of online lead brokers create perfect conditions for dishonest operators. If you have read moving company scams Reddit threads in r/Miami, r/florida, or r/moving, you have seen the same horror stories: lowball quotes that triple on moving day, belongings held hostage for cash payment, and crews that never show up at all.

This guide explains how to verify a moving company, recognize bait and switch movers, and book licensed movers Florida residents can trust — before you sign anything.

Common Moving Scams in South Florida

The Lowball Estimate

A company quotes $300 over the phone for a job that clearly takes six hours. On move day they claim you have "far more items than disclosed" and demand $900 before unloading. This classic bait and switch movers tactic preys on people who never received an in-home or video inventory.

The Hostage Load

After loading the truck, movers refuse to deliver unless you pay an inflated balance in cash. Interstate moves see this most often, but local scam artists use similar pressure tactics when you are on a tight lease deadline.

The Broker Shell Game

You book with one brand name; a different unvetted crew arrives in an unmarked truck. Lead-generation websites sell your information to the lowest bidder. You have little recourse because the company you researched is not the company that showed up.

The Unlicensed Operator

Anyone with a truck and a website can advertise. Without proper licensing and insurance, your belongings have zero protection and you may have no valid claim if items are damaged or stolen.

Red Flags Reddit Users Warn About

Across moving company scams Reddit discussions, these warning signs appear again and again:

  • No physical business address — only a PO box or generic location
  • Large deposit required upfront, especially via wire transfer or cryptocurrency
  • Refusal to provide a written estimate or contract
  • Company name that closely mimics a well-known national brand
  • Reviews that spike suddenly with generic five-star praise
  • No USDOT number for interstate moves or no Florida intrastate license
  • Estimator who does not ask detailed questions about your inventory
  • Price far below every other licensed movers Florida quote you received

If three or more red flags appear, keep looking. Legitimate companies welcome scrutiny.

How to Verify a Moving Company in Florida

Before you book, complete this verification checklist:

  • Check Florida licensing. Intrastate movers must be registered with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. Ask for the license number and verify it.
  • Confirm insurance. Request proof of liability and workers' compensation coverage. Buildings often require a certificate of insurance — a real company provides one quickly.
  • Research the business. Look up the company on BBB, Google Reviews, and Yelp. Search the owner name plus "complaint" or "scam."
  • Visit the website critically. Legitimate sites list a real address, phone number, and service area — not just a generic contact form.
  • Get everything in writing. Estimates, policies, cancellation terms, and payment methods should be documented before move day.
  • Pay wisely. Avoid large cash deposits. Pay agreed amounts with credit card or check when possible for a paper trail.

Bait-and-Switch Pricing: How to Protect Yourself

The best defense against inflated move-day bills is a thorough pre-move survey. Walk the estimator through every room, closet, garage, and patio. Mention stairs, elevator restrictions, and heavy items like pianos or gun safes.

Compare quotes apples to apples. One company may include travel time and materials; another may add them later. Understand whether your job is hourly or flat-rate and what triggers additional charges. Read our local move cost guide for realistic pricing context.

On move day, if the crew claims your inventory exceeds the estimate by a large margin, stop before signing any new authorization. Contact the office manager. Document everything with photos. You have more leverage before the truck is fully loaded than after.

What to Do If You Are Scammed

If you believe you are a victim of a Florida moving scam:

  • File a complaint with the Florida Attorney General's office
  • Report to the Better Business Bureau
  • Contact local law enforcement if belongings are being withheld
  • Dispute unauthorized credit card charges with your bank
  • Document all communications, contracts, and photos of damaged items

Prevention is always easier than recovery. Taking thirty minutes to verify a moving company saves thousands in stress and loss.
