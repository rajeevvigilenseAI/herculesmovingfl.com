Moving to Boca Raton: A Local Mover's Guide

October 2026  |  Boca Raton, Palm Beach County

Ask three people why they moved to Boca Raton and you may get three different answers. One wanted a country-club community with a golf course out back. One came for Florida Atlantic University. One sold the family house up north, or down the road, and moved into a smaller place in a 55+ community.

Those are very different moves, and Boca handles all of them. This guide starts with the most useful thing to understand about the area, the split between east and west Boca, then works through each type of move and what tends to go right or wrong.

East Boca vs west Boca

Locals use "east Boca" and "west Boca" constantly, and the difference matters for a move.

East Boca generally means the area east of I-95 toward the ocean. It includes downtown and Mizner Park, the older neighborhoods around Palmetto Park Road and Camino Real, waterfront homes along the Intracoastal and condo buildings along A1A. Streets are more established, lots vary a lot in size, and beachside buildings bring the usual condo logistics: elevator reservations, loading areas and set move hours.

West Boca usually refers to the large area west of the city's core, out past Florida's Turnpike toward US-441. Much of it is actually outside city limits in unincorporated Palm Beach County, even though the mailing address says Boca Raton. It is dominated by planned communities, many of them gated, with clubhouses, golf courses and lakes. Glades Road, Yamato Road, Palmetto Park Road and Clint Moore Road carry most of the east-west traffic.

Why this matters: if you are in unincorporated west Boca, rules about things like truck parking and service may come from your HOA and the county rather than the city. Ask your community which applies.

Gated and country-club communities

A large share of Boca moves end at a guard gate. Country-club communities in particular can be well organized but particular about how moves happen. Before move day, it helps to ask the management office:

  • How to register your movers with the gate, and how far ahead
  • Which days and hours moves are allowed
  • Where a large truck should park, and whether it can stay on the street during the move
  • Whether a Certificate of Insurance is required
  • Whether there are rules about leaving boxes or packing material out for pickup

We wrote a full checklist for this in our Palm Beach County gated community move guide. Most of it applies directly to Boca.

55+ communities

Boca Raton and the surrounding area have many active adult and 55+ communities. Moves into them often come with a few extra layers:

  1. Association approval. Many 55+ associations review new residents before closing or move-in. Start early so the approval is in hand before the movers are booked.
  2. Smaller floor plans. Villas, condos and single-story homes in these communities are often smaller than the house you are leaving. Measure rooms and decide on furniture before you pack.
  3. Help from family. Adult children often help coordinate from a distance. Share the move plan and inventory with them so everyone is working from the same list.
  4. Setup on arrival. Ask for beds to be assembled and key furniture placed first so the first night in the new home is comfortable.

Downsizing moves

Downsizing is one of the most common moves in Boca, and the hardest part happens before the truck arrives. Moving furniture that will not fit costs time and money, so decide early what goes, what gets sold or donated and what goes to family.

Some practical habits help:

  • Use a floor plan of the new home and tape off large pieces if you are unsure they will fit.
  • Pick the heirlooms that matter most, and flag them for extra wrapping.
  • Schedule donation pickups or family hand-offs before move day, not after.
  • If you only need a few pieces moved, a smaller move may be the right fit.

FAU and student moves

Florida Atlantic University's main campus sits in Boca Raton off Glades Road, and Lynn University is in town as well. Student moves cluster around the start and end of each semester, and apartments near campus get busy at the same time.

If you are moving a student, keep it simple. Pack in small, sturdy boxes, label each one with the room and the student's name, and check the apartment complex's move-in rules before you arrive. Many complexes set move-in times or limit where trucks can park. A small move is often all a dorm-to-apartment or apartment-to-apartment move needs.

Roads, traffic and timing

I-95 and Florida's Turnpike are the main north-south routes through Boca, with US-1 and A1A on the east side and US-441 on the west. Glades Road near the interstate and around the shopping areas is one of the busiest stretches in town. Early starts help with traffic and with summer heat, and hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. Winter is a busy season here too, as seasonal residents arrive and leave.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between east Boca and west Boca?

East Boca is generally the area east of I-95, including downtown, older neighborhoods and beach condos. West Boca is mostly planned and gated communities farther inland, and much of it sits in unincorporated Palm Beach County despite the Boca Raton address.

Do 55+ communities in Boca Raton require approval before I move in?

Many do. Ask the association about its approval process early so it is finished before you book your movers.

Can you move a student into an apartment near FAU?

Yes. Student moves are usually small moves, and we recommend checking the complex's move-in hours and truck parking rules before the day.

How do I get a moving truck through a Boca Raton guard gate?

Register your movers with the gate or management office ahead of time, using whatever method your community requires. Confirm allowed move hours at the same time.
