Moving in Miami-Dade County: The Big Picture

October 2026  |  Miami-Dade County

Miami-Dade is not one place. It is a downtown of glass towers, a string of barrier islands joined to the mainland by causeways, and a wide spread of suburbs that runs west toward the Everglades and south toward Homestead. A move from Brickell to Kendall and a move from Hialeah to Key Biscayne happen in the same county, but they feel like different jobs.

This guide steps back from any single city. It covers how the county is laid out, how its highways and bridges decide how long your move takes, and how to think about where in Miami-Dade you actually want to live. When you are ready to zoom in, the city guides linked below go deeper.

Three Miami-Dades in one county

Most moves in the county fall into one of three settings, and each one asks for a different plan.

The urban core: Downtown, Brickell, Edgewater and nearby

The center of the county is vertical. Brickell, Downtown, Edgewater and the area around Midtown are dominated by condo and rental towers, with older low-rise neighborhoods like Little Havana and Coconut Grove close by. Here the building sets the schedule. Expect reserved elevator windows, loading dock time slots and a Certificate of Insurance (COI) request before the truck is allowed in. Street parking for a moving truck is tight, so the loading dock is usually the only realistic option.

If your move starts or ends in a tower, read our high-rise condo moving guide before you book anything. For a closer look at the city itself, the Moving to Miami guide breaks down neighborhoods inside city limits.

The beaches and barrier islands

Miami Beach, Surfside, Bal Harbour, Sunny Isles Beach and Key Biscayne sit on islands or narrow strips of land east of Biscayne Bay. Getting there means a causeway: the MacArthur, the Julia Tuttle (I-195), the Venetian, the 79th Street Causeway and others to the north, or the Rickenbacker for Key Biscayne. There are only a handful of ways on and off, so one accident or a big event on the beach can slow every crossing at once.

Housing on the islands is a mix of oceanfront towers, mid-century low-rise apartments and single-family homes on the bay side. Many older buildings have small elevators or none at all, and loading areas can be shared with deliveries. Ask your building or HOA early about move-in hours, truck size and where a truck can stand while it is loaded.

The suburban west and south

West of the Palmetto Expressway and south of the city, the county opens up into single-family homes, townhouse communities and garden apartments. Doral, Sweetwater and Westchester to the west, Kendall and its neighbors to the southwest, and Pinecrest, Palmetto Bay, Cutler Bay and Homestead further south. To the north, Hialeah, Miami Lakes and Miami Gardens have their own mix of older homes and newer planned communities.

These moves are usually about volume rather than elevators: full houses, garages, patio furniture and sometimes a piano or safe. Gated townhouse communities may want the truck registered at the gate in advance. Doral and Coral Gables each have a guide of their own: see Moving to Doral and Moving to Coral Gables.

How geography and highways shape a Miami-Dade move

The county is long from north to south and pinned between Biscayne Bay and the Everglades. That shape funnels traffic onto a few main routes, and the route usually matters more than the mileage.

  • I-95 runs north-south along the east side and ends just south of Downtown, where traffic continues onto US-1. It is the main link between the urban core and Broward.
  • SR 836 (Dolphin Expressway) is the main east-west route between Downtown, the airport area and Doral.
  • SR 826 (Palmetto Expressway) loops through the middle of the county, connecting Kendall, the airport area, Hialeah and Miami Lakes.
  • Florida's Turnpike (the Homestead Extension) runs along the western suburbs from the Broward line down to Homestead and Florida City.
  • US-1 carries a lot of local traffic south through Coconut Grove, Coral Gables, Pinecrest and on toward Homestead.

A few practical habits follow from that layout. Plan island moves around the causeway, not the clock alone. Give a Kendall-to-Downtown move extra time on weekday mornings, when the expressways into the core fill up. If you are moving from the far south of the county to the north end, treat it like a longer move and start early so unloading does not run into evening building hours.

Weather and timing

Summer afternoons bring heat and frequent rain, and hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. Morning start times help on both counts. Many towers also limit moves to weekdays or certain hours, so lock in your elevator reservation before you lock in a date. How to choose where in Miami-Dade to live

People move within the county for very different reasons, so start with how you want to live, then narrow down the map.

  1. Commute first. Pick the highway you will use every day and look along it. Living near the same corridor as your job saves more time than living closer as the crow flies.
  2. Decide on building or yard. Towers and island condos trade space for amenities and walkability. The western and southern suburbs trade walkability for square footage and parking.
  3. Think about water. Bay and ocean views come with causeway traffic and building rules. Inland areas are easier on move day and in daily errands.
  4. Visit at the hour you will travel. Drive the route at rush hour before you sign a lease. A neighborhood that looks close on a Sunday can feel far on a Tuesday.
  5. Read the building or HOA rules. Move-in fees, deposits, COI requirements and allowed hours vary from one association to the next.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a move across Miami-Dade County take?

It depends far more on traffic, building rules and how much you own than on miles. A move between the south end of the county and the beaches can take noticeably longer at rush hour, so morning starts usually help.

Do I need a Certificate of Insurance to move into a Miami-Dade condo?

Many condo and rental towers ask for one before a moving truck can use the loading dock or elevator. Ask your building management what they require, and Hercules Local Movers can coordinate the COI with them.

Is it harder to move onto Miami Beach or Key Biscayne?

Not harder, but it takes more planning. There are only a few causeways on and off the islands, and many buildings have limited loading areas and set move hours, so confirm those details early.

Which part of Miami-Dade is best for families who need more space?

Families looking for yards and parking often look at the western and southern suburbs, such as Kendall, Doral or Pinecrest, while people who want walkability tend to choose the urban core. Drive your daily route at rush hour before deciding.
