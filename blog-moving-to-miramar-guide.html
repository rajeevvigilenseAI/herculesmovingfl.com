Moving to Miramar: A Local Mover's Guide

October 2026  |  Miramar, Broward County

Miramar sits in southwest Broward County, right on the Miami-Dade line, and for a lot of households that location is the whole point. You can reach jobs in Miami or Fort Lauderdale without living in either downtown, and you get more house and more yard for the trouble.

Most of the city's newer neighborhoods were built as planned communities, so a move here usually means working with an HOA, a guard gate or both. This guide walks through what that looks like on move day, how the road network affects timing, and how to plan a family-size move without surprises.

East Miramar and west Miramar are different places

Think of Miramar in two halves. The eastern part, closer to US-441 and I-95, is older. You will find mid-century single-family homes on standard grid streets, many without an HOA, along with some townhome and apartment complexes. Moves here look like typical suburban house moves: a driveway, a front door and a few steps.

Farther west, the city opens up into large planned developments built around lakes, with names such as Silver Lakes, Monarch Lakes, Sunset Lakes and Riviera Isles. These are the communities most people picture when they think of Miramar. They tend to have curving streets, cul-de-sacs, community clubhouses and a front gate or entry feature. Out toward I-75, newer communities continue, along with business parks and the Miramar Town Center area.

What HOA communities usually expect

Every association sets its own rules, and they change, so treat the list below as questions to ask rather than rules that definitely apply to you.

  • Gate access. Ask how the gate will know your movers are coming. Some communities want names on a guest list, others want a call from the resident.
  • Move hours. Some HOAs limit moving to certain days or hours. Confirm before you lock in a date.
  • Truck parking. Ask whether a large truck may park on the street, whether it must stay off the grass and whether there are weight or size limits on private roads.
  • Insurance paperwork. A few associations ask for a Certificate of Insurance from the mover. We can coordinate that directly.
  • Approval for renters. If you are renting, your lease approval may need to clear the association before move-in day.

The simplest step is to email or call the property management company a couple of weeks ahead. It saves a lot of standing around at the gate.

Family homes mean family-size inventories

Miramar is a family town, and the houses reflect it. Three- and four-bedroom homes with garages are common, and garages tend to fill up with bikes, tools, sports equipment, holiday decorations and outdoor furniture. Those items take more time to load than people expect, because they are odd shapes that do not stack.

A few ways to keep a house move on track:

  1. Clear the garage first. Sort, donate or toss before packing day so the crew is not loading things you planned to get rid of.
  2. Label by room. In two-story homes, clear labels save trips up and down the stairs at the new place.
  3. Plan the kids' rooms last out, first in. Loading those rooms last means they come off the truck first, and bedtime on day one is easier.
  4. Ask about disassembly. Bunk beds, cribs and large dining tables often need to come apart. Basic disassembly and reassembly is included with Hercules Local Movers, but tell us about it in advance.

Commuting and the roads that shape move day

Location is the main reason many people choose Miramar. Florida's Turnpike runs north and south through the city, I-75 runs along the west side toward Weston and Fort Lauderdale, and I-95 is a short drive east. Miramar Parkway and Pembroke Road are the main east-west roads, and Red Road, University Drive and Flamingo Road carry a lot of north-south traffic between communities.

That same connectivity means rush hour is real. If you are moving from Miami-Dade, the Turnpike and I-95 corridors into Broward can back up in the morning. From Fort Lauderdale, I-595 to I-75 or the Turnpike is often the cleanest route, but it slows down at peak times as well. A start time that dodges the morning commute helps, and so does packing the truck the night before if your schedule allows it.

Who tends to move to Miramar

The common stories are familiar ones. A family outgrows a Miami apartment and wants a yard. A couple with jobs in two counties picks the middle. Renters in a townhome move into a single-family house a few streets away once they are ready to buy. Moves within Miramar itself are common too, often from an older east-side home into a newer community in the west.

Short local moves like that can still be a full day of work if it is a big house, so plan for it the same way you would a longer move.

Timing, weather and estimates

Summer brings heat and afternoon storms, and hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. Early starts help on both counts. School calendars matter here as well, since many families try to move in early summer before the new school year. Book early if you are aiming for those weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need HOA approval to move into a Miramar community?

Many planned communities in Miramar have an HOA, and some require approval for new residents or renters before move-in. Check with the property manager early so the approval does not hold up your move date.

Will the guard gate let a moving truck in?

Usually, as long as the gate knows to expect it. Ask your community how to register your movers, whether by guest list, resident call or another method.

Is Miramar a good place to live if I work in Miami or Fort Lauderdale?

Many commuters choose it for that reason. Florida's Turnpike, I-75 and I-95 all connect Miramar to both cities, though rush hour traffic can be heavy in either direction.

How long does it take to move a family home in Miramar?

It depends on the size of the home, how much is in the garage and how far you are going. A written estimate based on your inventory is the best way to get a realistic time frame.

What is the best time of year to move to Miramar?

Many families aim for early summer to line up with school schedules, which makes those weeks busy. Cooler months are more comfortable, and early morning starts help year-round.
