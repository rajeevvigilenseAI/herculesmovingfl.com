Moving in Palm Beach County: From Boca Raton to Jupiter

October 2026  |  Palm Beach County

Palm Beach County is long. It stretches from Boca Raton at the Broward line all the way up past Jupiter and Tequesta, and west to the farm towns near Lake Okeechobee. A move inside the county can be a ten-minute hop inside a gated community or a trip that covers a good part of the coast. That spread, plus the number of gated, 55+ and seasonal households, makes it a different kind of moving market from Miami-Dade or Broward.

This guide is the wide view: how the county is laid out, what to expect in gated and age-restricted communities, how seasonal residents usually time their moves, and how the island towns differ from the mainland. The Palm Beach and Boca Raton guides cover those places in more detail.

A county measured north to south

It helps to think of the county as a series of bands along the coast, with suburbs reaching inland behind each one.

  • South county: Boca Raton, Highland Beach, Delray Beach and Boynton Beach. Downtown districts near the coast, large gated and country club communities to the west.
  • Central county: Lake Worth Beach, Lantana, Palm Springs, West Palm Beach and, across the Intracoastal, the Town of Palm Beach. Older neighborhoods, a downtown with condo towers and the county's main urban center.
  • North county: Riviera Beach, North Palm Beach, Palm Beach Gardens, Juno Beach, Jupiter and Tequesta. A mix of waterfront homes, golf communities and newer subdivisions.
  • The western communities: Wellington, Royal Palm Beach, Loxahatchee and Lake Worth's western suburbs, with larger lots and equestrian properties. Further west, the Glades towns near Lake Okeechobee are rural and far from the coast.

Because the county is so long, the first question for any move is how far apart the two addresses really are. A Boca to Jupiter move is a very different day from a move within Delray Beach.

Roads that run the length of the county

I-95 and Florida's Turnpike both run the full north-south length of the county, a few miles apart. The Turnpike sits further west and often suits moves to or from the western communities, while I-95 is closer to the coastal downtowns. US-1 and A1A carry local traffic along the coast and the barrier islands, with lots of lights and slower speeds.

East-west boulevards do much of the work at each end of a move: Glades Road in Boca, Atlantic Avenue in Delray, Okeechobee Boulevard and Southern Boulevard around West Palm Beach, and PGA Boulevard in Palm Beach Gardens. Expect those roads to be busy near the interstate interchanges and malls, especially during winter when the population swells.

Gated and 55+ communities

Gated communities are a defining feature of Palm Beach County, especially west of I-95 in Boca Raton, Delray Beach, Boynton Beach, Lake Worth and Wellington, and in the golf communities up north. Many are age-restricted 55+ communities. Moving into or out of one usually goes smoothly if you handle a few things ahead of time:

  1. Gate access. Ask how the guard gate wants the moving company listed, and give them the date and approximate arrival time.
  2. Move hours and days. Some communities limit moves to certain hours or keep weekends quiet. Confirm before you book.
  3. Insurance paperwork. A Certificate of Insurance (COI) may be required, especially in condo-style buildings.
  4. Truck size and parking. Some communities have size limits, narrow private roads or rules about blocking driveways.
  5. Downsizing. Many 55+ moves go from a larger house to a smaller villa or condo. Decide what goes before move day so you only pay to move what fits.

Seasonal residents and timing

A lot of Palm Beach County households are seasonal. Many residents spend the winter here and the summer somewhere else, and that rhythm affects moving. Winter brings heavier traffic, busier roads near the coast and more competition for weekend dates in condo buildings. Late spring is often when seasonal residents close up homes, downsize or move between properties.

Summer brings fewer people but more heat and afternoon storms, and hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. If you are flexible, an early morning start on a weekday is usually the easiest move in any season. Our best time to move in South Florida guide covers the tradeoffs.

If you are a part-time resident moving only a few pieces between a seasonal condo and another home in the county, a small move may be all you need.

Island vs mainland

The barrier islands, including the Town of Palm Beach, Highland Beach, Manalapan and parts of Jupiter and Juno Beach, feel very different from the mainland. They are reached by bridges over the Intracoastal, and some of those bridges open for boats. Homes range from oceanfront condos to large estates, and many have specific rules from the town, the building or the association about when trucks can be on site and where they can park.

Estate homes often hold fine art, antiques and pianos, which need careful handling and sometimes custom crating. For the Town of Palm Beach in particular, see our Moving to Palm Beach guide. On the mainland, moves are more varied: towers in downtown West Palm Beach, older bungalows in Lake Worth Beach, and single-family homes in suburban subdivisions.

Picking where in the county to live

Start with where you will spend most of your days. If your work or family is in Broward, south county keeps the commute short, and the Moving to Boca Raton guide is a good next read. If you want a downtown with towers and walkable streets, look at West Palm Beach or Delray Beach. For space and horses, the western communities fit. North county suits people who want a quieter coast and plenty of golf and boating.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a move from Boca Raton to Jupiter take?

It covers a good part of the county, so the drive alone adds meaningful time to the day. The total depends on how much you are moving, traffic on I-95 or the Turnpike and the access rules at both addresses.

What do I need to move into a 55+ or gated community in Palm Beach County?

Rules vary by community, but common steps include listing the movers with the guard gate, confirming allowed move hours and providing a Certificate of Insurance if asked. Check with your HOA before you book a date.

When do seasonal residents usually move?

Many seasonal residents move in late spring when they close up for the summer, or in the fall before they return for winter. Winter is the busiest time on the roads, so plan building reservations early.

Is moving onto the Town of Palm Beach different from moving on the mainland?

Yes. You cross a bridge over the Intracoastal, and the town, building or association may have rules about truck timing and parking. Estate homes also tend to have more fine art and antiques that need extra care.
