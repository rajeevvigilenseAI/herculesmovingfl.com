High-Rise & Condo Moving in Miami & Fort Lauderdale: What to Expect

September 2026  |  Miami and Fort Lauderdale

High-rise moves in Brickell, downtown Miami, and along Las Olas run on the building's rules, not yours. The inventory is only half the job. The other half is a Certificate of Insurance, a freight elevator that someone else may already own for the afternoon, and a dock that closes whether or not the last nightstand is upstairs. Knowing those rules ahead of time is the difference between a day that finishes and a day that stalls at security.

This is not the same problem as a Palm Beach gated community (gate lists and HOA hours) or a small-move minimum. It is tower logistics. Miami's lead is mixed building types and beach access. Fort Lauderdale's lead is Las Olas and downtown high-rises that will not let a truck dock without a current COI. Both cities share the same paperwork stack once you are above the second floor.

The Certificate of Insurance (COI)

A COI is a one-page proof from the mover's insurance company. It names the building and often the management company, states the liability limits, and sometimes lists additional insured language the packet requires. Front desk and loading-dock staff use it as a yes/no. No COI, no dock. No dock, no move, even if the elevator is reserved.

What management needs is specific. The named entity must match the legal name in the condo docs, not the marketing name on the awning. Limits are often set by the association, not by what a mover thinks is "enough." Send the packet to your mover as soon as you have it. Turnaround is usually days, not minutes, because the insurer issues the certificate, not the crew on site.

Freight elevator reservations

Passenger elevators are for residents. Freight is for pads, dollies, and time. You reserve a window. You pad it. A "10 to 2" that actually means the cab is yours until 1:40 because the next resident's reservation is firm will wreck a quote that assumed four open hours. Single-elevator buildings are stricter. If that cab is the only way to your floor, month-end Saturdays disappear first.

Book as soon as the date is real, and at least as far ahead as the packet says. Some Miami towers want two or three weeks. Some Fort Lauderdale buildings will take a week if the COI is already on file. Do not assume the mover can "call downstairs" on the morning of. They can call. The answer is often no.

Loading docks, staging, and time windows

Towers control where a truck may stand and for how long. A dock with a height cap, a shared alley, or a valet lane that cannot be blocked will change how the crew stages. Many buildings ban evening moves or limit trucks to weekdays. That is independent of the mover's own hours. We are open 7 days, 6:30 AM-10:00 PM. If the tower only allows 9 to 12 on weekdays, that is the job.

Street parking is not a backup plan in Brickell or on Las Olas. If the dock is booked, you wait or you reschedule. A crew that knows those buildings plans the reservation and the load sequence so the heavy pieces use the freight while you still have it.

A pre-move checklist for high-rise residents

  1. Download or request the move-in packet the week you get keys or a closing date.
  2. Highlight COI wording, elevator lead time, allowed hours, and dock rules.
  3. Send the packet to your mover before you lock the calendar.
  4. Reserve the freight elevator with padding on both sides of the estimate.
  5. Confirm the COI was received. Ask for written confirmation, not a verbal "it should be fine."
  6. Share unit number, loading-dock instructions, and a working phone number for security to call.
  7. Measure beds and sofas against elevator cab dimensions if the building is older.
  8. Protect the hold: be downstairs when the truck is due. A missed dock slot is often gone.

High-rise moving FAQs

What is a COI and why does my building need one?
A Certificate of Insurance is a document from the mover's insurer showing the building and management as interested parties, at the liability limits the tower requires. Security uses it to confirm the crew is covered before a truck is allowed on the dock or a freight elevator is turned over.
How early should I book a freight elevator?
As soon as your move date is real, and at least as far ahead as management's packet requires. Many Miami and Fort Lauderdale towers want one to three weeks. Single-elevator buildings fill even faster at month-end.
Can you move on weekends or in the evening?
Hercules Local Movers is open 7 days, 6:30 AM-10:00 PM. Whether the building allows a weekend or evening window is a separate rule. We schedule inside the hours management publishes.
What if my building has a narrow move-in window?
We plan crew size and staging around that window so the heavy pieces hit the freight elevator while it is yours. A two-hour dock slot is workable when the COI and reservation are already done. It is not workable if paperwork starts when the truck arrives.

Tower moves are paperwork first, furniture second. Get the certificate on file, get the elevator in writing, then get your free estimate.
