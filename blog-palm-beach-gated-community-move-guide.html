Moving Into a Gated Community in Palm Beach County: What to Arrange First

September 2026  |  Palm Beach County

Palm Beach County has more gated and HOA-managed communities than almost anywhere in Florida, and each one writes its own move-in rules. Miss a step and the truck sits at the gate while a guard waits on a name that is not on the list. That is not a rare edge case in Boca, Delray, Boynton, Wellington, or the 55+ neighborhoods west of the Turnpike. It is a normal Tuesday. This is the checklist to run before anyone loads a wardrobe box.

Why gated-community moves need advance planning

A house move on an open street is mostly inventory and time. A gated move adds a second project: permission. The association decides which days a truck may enter, which gate it uses, whether the crew must register vehicles, and whether a Certificate of Insurance has to be on file before the first dolly crosses the sidewalk. Property managers are protecting roads, landscaping, and other residents. They are not set up to make exceptions at 8 a.m. because the mover "didn't know."

Palm Beach County's housing mix makes this more common than in Miami Beach or downtown Fort Lauderdale. Single-family homes inside guard gates, coach homes, and 55+ communities are the default in large parts of the county. If your new address has a gate arm, assume there is a packet. Ask for it the week you sign, not the night before the move.

The move-in requirements to check with your HOA

Call or email the management office and get answers in writing. Rules differ by community, but the categories repeat.

Approved move-in days and hours

Many associations block Sundays. Some block all weekends. Some allow Saturday morning only. A few limit moves to weekdays before 4 p.m. If your lease starts on a Saturday and the community does not allow Saturday trucks, you need a plan (temporary storage, a weekday elevator, or a different first night) before you book the crew.

Gate registration and vehicle check-in

Guards typically want the company name, a contact on site, plate numbers or a truck description, and sometimes photo ID for each mover. Some communities issue day passes. Others want the list 24 to 48 hours ahead. A crew that arrives unlisted does not argue its way in. It waits, or it leaves and comes back, and you pay for that time.

Certificate of Insurance (COI)

Gated HOAs often want the same COI high-rises want: the association and the management company listed as additional interest, with liability limits the packet specifies. This is paperwork between the mover's insurer and the association, not something you print at a copy shop the morning of. Give the building's exact legal name as it appears in the documents. A mismatched named insured is a rejected COI.

Designated entrances, loading zones, and elevators

Some communities ban moving trucks from the front gate and send them to a service entrance. Condos and coach-home buildings may still require a freight elevator reservation even though you are "in a gated community," not a Brickell tower. Ask where the truck may stand and how long it may stay. Parking on a private street without permission is how you get a stop-work from security.

Deposits and move fees

A number of associations charge a move-in fee or hold a refundable elevator deposit. That charge is from the HOA to you, not from the mover. Budget for it and ask whether a check or a portal payment is required before the gate will clear the truck.

A simple pre-move checklist

  1. Request the move-in packet from management as soon as you have a unit number.
  2. Highlight allowed days, hours, and the cutoff for submitting names and the COI.
  3. Send those dates to your mover before you lock a calendar day.
  4. Confirm the exact additional-insured wording for the COI and who must receive it.
  5. Ask which gate or service entrance the truck must use, and whether a parking pass is required.
  6. If there is an elevator, reserve it for a window that matches the association's hours, with padding on both sides.
  7. Pay any HOA move fee or deposit and keep the receipt on your phone.
  8. The day before, reconfirm the guest list with the gate and text your mover the entry instructions.
  9. On move morning, be reachable. Guards call the resident, not the truck, when a name does not match.
